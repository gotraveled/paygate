Stripe is a payment facilitator headquartered in United States. It underwrites a shared merchant pool, letting most standard businesses start processing quickly without a dedicated acquiring relationship.

Its offering is aimed at online businesses of all sizes, SaaS, marketplaces, and developers needing API-first payments.

In practical terms, the same pooled structure means risk controls are largely automated, and accounts flagged by those controls can face holds with limited recourse — a trade-off worth weighing before applying.

## Where you can apply

Stripe accepts merchant applications from businesses registered in Australia, Canada, United Kingdom, Malaysia, and United States.

Applications from United Arab Emirates, Germany, India, Nigeria, and Singapore are conditionally available — approval may depend on entity type, product, or an invitation rather than open signup.

Entity requirements: A registered business in a Stripe-supported country; sole proprietors supported in many countries.

India availability is Preview/invite-based; some regions are served only via the extended network. US fee schedule differs from region-specific pricing pages.

Separate questions — whether your customers can pay, and where funds can settle — are not covered by application eligibility and are not verified in our data for this provider.

## Industries it serves

Standard acceptance is recorded for SaaS & Software, Ecommerce & Retail, Digital Products, Subscription Businesses, Freelancers & Agencies, and Marketplaces.

Gaming & Gambling may be accepted case-by-case — expect extra underwriting questions or documentation.

Travel, Supplements & Nutraceuticals, Forex & Financial Services, and Crypto & Digital Assets appear on the provider's restricted-businesses terms, which usually means prior written approval or an outright decline depending on specifics.

Adult Content is prohibited; applying under this category risks termination and frozen funds.

Industry lists describe categories, not guarantees: two businesses in the same industry can receive different decisions based on underwriting.

## Fees and other costs

- **Chargeback / dispute fee** — $15 (US)

- **Fixed fee per charge (US)** — $0.30. Part of the 2.9% + $0.30 online rate

- **Online card payments (US)** — 2.9%. Plus $0.30 fixed per transaction; varies by country and method

Both a percentage and a fixed per-transaction charge apply, so your blended rate rises as average ticket size falls — merchants selling low-priced items should model this before committing.

## Reserves, settlements, and payouts

- **Payout schedule** — Standard payouts on a rolling schedule (typically 2 business days in the US; varies by country and risk); around 2 days; First payout can take longer; schedule varies by country

- **Rolling reserve** — Reserves may be applied case-by-case for higher-risk accounts; terms set during underwriting; Not a fixed public percentage

If a rolling reserve applies to your account, a slice of each payout is withheld — for a business running on thin margins this is a cash-flow commitment, not an abstract risk term. Get the percentage and release schedule in writing.

## Integration and payment capabilities

Payment flows: one-time payments, recurring / subscriptions, and marketplace / split payments.

Integration surface includes a developer API, hosted checkout pages, invoicing, marketplace/split-payment tooling, ecommerce plugins, and recurring billing tools.

## Applying

The published onboarding path runs:

1. Create an account on the provider website
2. Submit business and owner details for verification
3. Connect a bank account for payouts
4. Wait for underwriting review and account approval
5. Integrate and start processing

Expect to provide:

- Business registration details (entity name, number, country)
- Owner / director identity documents (KYC)
- Business bank account details
- Website or product information

## Strengths and drawbacks

Points in its favor:

- Excellent developer APIs and documentation
- Broad product suite (Billing, Connect, Radar)
- Fast onboarding for standard businesses

Points against it:

- Account stability relies on automated risk review
- High-risk and restricted categories often declined
- Reserves/holds can be applied without much warning

These are structural observations from the provider's model and published terms — not user reviews or satisfaction ratings, which we do not collect or fabricate.

## Questions to ask before applying

- Is my business entity in United Arab Emirates actually eligible, and under what conditions?
- Will underwriting accept my specific business model — not just my broad industry label?
- What reserve percentage applies to my account, and when is it released?
- What are the payout schedule and currency options for my settlement country?
