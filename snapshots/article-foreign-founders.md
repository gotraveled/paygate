## The core problem

Most US-based payment providers underwrite against a US entity and a US-resident owner. Foreign founders typically need a US entity first, then a provider that will accept a non-resident owner — two separate gates that fail independently.

## Payment providers on record for foreign founders

- **[2Checkout (Verifone)](/providers/2checkout-verifone)** — merchant of record
- **[Stripe](/providers/stripe)** — payment facilitator
- **[Flutterwave](/providers/flutterwave)** — payment gateway

The other route is a Merchant of Record, which sells under its own entity and largely sidesteps residency requirements.

## Formation providers

- **[Stripe Atlas](/providers/stripe-atlas)** — Delaware company formation (C-corp or LLC) for global founders, from Stripe.
- **[Firstbase](/providers/firstbase)** — Company formation and compliance platform for global founders.
- **[doola](/providers/doola)** — US LLC/C-corp formation with bookkeeping and compliance add-ons for global founders.
- **[Clerky](/providers/clerky)** — Legal formation service used heavily by startups for Delaware incorporations.
- **[Northwest Registered Agent](/providers/northwest-registered-agent)** — Registered agent and LLC/corporation formation in every US state.
- **[Bizee](/providers/bizee)** — Low-cost US LLC formation (formerly Incfile) with a free basic tier.

## What to verify before applying

- Whether the provider underwrites the OWNER, the entity, or both.
- Whether a US EIN alone is enough, or a US-resident signer is required.
- Which industries are excluded for foreign-owned entities specifically (often stricter than the general list).
- Payout banking: many require a US bank account in the entity's name.
